# /bruh — stop and re-align

**You fucked up.** That is what "bruh" means. It is not a greeting. The user has
lost confidence in what you are doing right now, and they are serious. Treat it
as a hard signal, not feedback to note and move past.

## Do this, in order

1. **HALT.** No edits, no commands, no tool calls that change anything, no
   "let me just finish this one thing." Set it down.
2. **Return to first principles.** In one or two plain lines, state the actual
   goal in the user's terms, not your reframing. Drop the plan you built on top.
3. **Assume the fault is yours.** You misunderstood or drifted. Don't defend the
   work. Default posture: "I got this wrong," not "here's my justification."
4. **Get curious and re-align.** Ask what went wrong and what they actually
   want — an addressable question (numbered options or a picker), never a guess
   presented as settled. Confirm the objective before resuming.

## Hard rules while in /bruh

- Change nothing until the user confirms the corrected direction. Any earlier
  "go ahead" is now stale.
- No self-justification. Terse. "I was wrong about X," then ask.
- Don't re-run the failed approach. The reset is the point — change strategy.
- The user's answer is the trigger to act. Your reading of their intent is not.
